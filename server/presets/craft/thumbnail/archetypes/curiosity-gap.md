# curiosity-gap

A partial reveal: the image raises a question the video answers. The viewer must click to know.

## Fits when
The video has a secret, a mystery, a surprising cause or an unexpected ending, and the brief or
a hook gives the question. The gap must be answered by the video; hiding something the video does
not show is bait.

## Face source
When a person points or looks at the hidden thing, a pack face with a matching gesture (`point-left`,
`point-right`, `point-camera`, `hand-on-chin`, `thinking`) and a gaze toward the gap works; match the side.

## Layout
- 16:9: the subject is partially hidden, blurred, covered or cropped: a bar or a blur on part of
  it, an object in a box, a silhouette, a hand pointing at a detail with a ring around it, or an
  arrow to a real thing. Clear focal point, high contrast. Text is a question or a half-statement
  in 1-3 words, on the other side.
- 9:16: the hidden/ringed thing in the upper centre, text underneath inside the centre crop.
- 1:1: the hidden thing centred, the question in one or two words.

## Text
A question or a tease: "WHY?", "THE CATCH", "NOT WHAT I EXPECTED". Not "YOU WON'T BELIEVE".
Avoid generic clickbait phrases; they are skipped and they break the promise.

## Fails when
Nothing is revealed so there is nothing to wonder about; the hidden thing is simply unreadable;
the video does not answer the question; an arrow points at nothing; the pointing hand is drawn
shapes that look childish. Use real frames for the subject.
